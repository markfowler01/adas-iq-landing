Consolidation heat rises as insurers double down on automation
Sunday, May 10 · #10

The collision industry's consolidation appetite just got more visible. Driven Brands is fielding a $18/share buyout offer (41% premium), while CCC's quarterly profits jumped 12% on the back of insurers aggressively adopting AI. What does it mean for you?

INDUSTRY

1. Driven Brands in play as activist pushes $18/share buyout

ADW Capital Management just publicly proposed acquiring Driven Brands for $18 per share—a 41% jump from the stock price at the time. This is a hostile play, and it signals appetite among big investors for roll-ups in the repair space. Watch this closely; if it lands, M&A ripples down to independent shop relationships fast.

Source: SCRS / Repairer Driven News →

INSURANCE

2. CCC's revenue surges 12% as insurers embrace AI

CCC reported Q1 revenue of $281.3M, up 12% YoY, driven largely by insurers deploying AI-powered claims tools. This matters because faster claims automation typically means tighter supplement negotiations and tighter timelines for your shop. Expect more pressure on pricing and cycle time.

Source: Collision Repair Magazine →

TRAINING

3. AAPEX 2026 registration opens for November Vegas show

Over 2,500 exhibitors are expected at AAPEX this November in Las Vegas, with expanded training offerings on the agenda. If you're looking to catch up on ADAS calibration best practices or scope out the latest OEM tools, this is the most efficient four days of the year.

Source: Collision Repair Magazine →

OEM

4. Mercedes-Benz Canada names new CEO

Susann Mayhead is now president and CEO of Mercedes-Benz Canada, overseeing OEM dealer relations and service strategy for the region. German luxury brands continue to tighten ADAS specs and calibration requirements; watch for potential new TSBs or equipment mandates under her tenure.

Source: Collision Repair Magazine →

INDUSTRY

5. Sherwin-Williams Canada bolsters auto leadership team

Two new regional and national sales managers are joining Sherwin-Williams' Canadian automotive division. As more shops standardize on premium refinish systems tied to OEM specs and ADAS-safe repair workflows, material supplier stability matters more than ever.

Source: Collision Repair Magazine →
