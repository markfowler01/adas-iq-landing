Parts cost crunch meets ADAS reckoning
Monday, May 11 · #5

OEM parts are getting expensive fast—and the industry just launched two major ADAS initiatives to handle the mess. Plus, a reality check on what insurers actually know about their AI investments.

INSURANCE

1. OEM parts spike; aftermarket flatlines

OEM new parts are seeing the largest price increases in the collision supply chain, while recycled and aftermarket parts remain flat, according to PartsTrader's Spring 2026 data. This widens the gap between what shops have to charge for OEM replacements versus alternatives—and it matters for your supplements and customer conversations when an insurer pushes back on part choices.

Source: PartsTrader / Repairer Driven News →

TRAINING

2. Industry launches two ADAS risk initiatives

SCRS and the Collision Industry Conference just rolled out coordinated efforts to tackle ADAS-related risks and opportunities in collision repair. The moves signal the industry is taking seriously what shops already know: ADAS complexity is exploding, and shops need better standards and community support to navigate it.

Source: SCRS →

INSURANCE

3. Insurers don't know if their AI actually works

42% of P&C insurance leaders haven't measured AI outcomes—even though 40% say AI is meeting expectations. Translation: shops are getting pushed into AI-powered estimate workflows without clarity on whether insurers' own systems actually deliver value. This matters when you're disputing supplements that an algorithm flagged.

Source: Capgemini / SCRS →

INSURANCE

4. Alternative parts hit 40% utilization record

LKQ reported alternative parts utilization reached 40% in Q1 2026—an all-time high. Insurers are leaning harder on recycled and aftermarket to control costs, even as OEM prices climb. Knowing your shop's APU trends versus market average helps you price work and negotiate with carriers.

Source: LKQ / Repairer Driven News →

INDUSTRY

5. Driven Brands faces $18/share buyout bid

Activist shareholder ADW Capital offered to take Driven Brands private at $18 per share—a 41% premium to market price. If the deal closes, it could reshape the consolidation landscape for collision groups, especially smaller shops tied to Driven's network.

Source: SCRS / Repairer Driven News →
